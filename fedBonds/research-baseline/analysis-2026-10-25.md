# CPI conditions and the fedBonds real-yield / breakeven curves

**Analysis cutoff: October 25, 2026.** Historical observations: June 2025 through October 2026.

## Executive conclusion

**There is no established winner across the three CPI conditions.** In the current archive, seasonal adjustment is much more useful than no adjustment. Choosing between settlement and forward, or between full SA and SA-decay, is substantially less certain.

On a common settlement-price basis, using held-out bonds rather than just the original fitting objective:

| CPI carry relative to recent trend | Eligible dates / CPI episodes | Lowest TIPS error | TIPS error, bp | Lowest BEI error | BEI error, bp |
|---|---:|---|---:|---|---:|
| Below trend | 9 / 5 | Forward / SA | 6.31 | Forward / SA | 8.00 |
| Near trend | 7 / 4 | Settlement / SA-decay | 2.85 | Settlement / SA-decay | 5.90 |
| Above trend | 17 / 8 | Settlement / SA | 4.56 | Settlement / SA | 6.87 |

These are **descriptive leaders, not validated selection rules**. The short-end rankings sometimes differ, quote-side results differ, and excluding the shutdown-sensitive period changes the above-trend leader to settlement / SA-decay.

There is also an important archive limitation: the replay used current TIPS metadata, which omits several bonds that were outstanding on historical dates. **Repairing that historical bond coverage should precede a strong conclusion about short-term BEI or real yields.**

## 1. What is being measured

This study measures:

1. How accurately each curve describes TIPS prices and associated real yields.
2. How accurately the resulting nominal-minus-real calculation describes a bond-level BEI proxy.
3. How sensitive the apparent curve shape is to valuation basis and CPI releases.

It does **not** measure realized investment returns, future inflation forecast accuracy, or a profitable trading strategy. A real yield is not a realized holding-period real return. A return study would additionally require trades, subsequent dirty prices, actual inflation accrual, coupon cash flows, financing, and an explicit holding period.

The six variants are:

- Settlement / unadjusted, SA, SA-decay.
- Forward to the historical `maxREFCPI` date / unadjusted, SA, SA-decay.

In this implementation, SA-decay credibility-weights projected maturity seasonal factors toward 1 as projection uncertainty increases. It does not decay away the already-known CPI change.

Both agency quote sides are evaluated separately before aggregation. Bid and ask are not counted as independent CPI observations.

### CPI regime definition

The question concerns **known inflation between the announcement and the maximum published reference-CPI date**, not simply the latest one-month headline CPI print.

For release date \(A\) and its historical maximum reference-CPI date \(H\):

```text
known carry = ln(REFCPI_NSA(H) / REFCPI_NSA(A)) / ((H - A) / 365)
trend       = (12 / 6) * ln(SA_CPI(m - 1) / SA_CPI(m - 7))
gap         = known carry - trend
```

Here `m` is the CPI reference month associated with the release. The trend uses six monthly changes ending **before** the newly released month. Rates use continuously compounded annualized units.

- **Below:** gap less than -0.50 percentage point.
- **Near:** gap within +/-0.50 percentage point.
- **Above:** gap greater than +0.50 percentage point.

The threshold is an explicit modeling choice, not an economic law. For example, the October 14, 2026 episode has 5.84% annualized known NSA carry against a 4.03% trailing trend, so it is above trend.

Every snapshot is labeled using the **latest release already available on that snapshot date**. A pre-release snapshot is not classified using the next, then-unknown CPI announcement. Matched pre/post comparisons are separately labeled by the intervening release.

This is **not a consensus-surprise classification**: no economist forecasts or market inflation-swap expectations are available in this study.

### Release exclusion and sample size

Of the 49 successfully backfilled dates:

- **33 dates** lie outside +/-3 Treasury-market trading days of any actual CPI release.
- **16 dates** fail that conservative exclusion rule.
- The retained dates represent **17 release episodes**, including the May 2025 release still governing the first June observation.
- There are **15 matched release events** with observations on both sides, each 4-10 trading days from the release.
- A sixteenth broad pair is possible for December 2025, but its pre-observation is November 21, too distant for the primary paired-event interpretation.

The seventh trading day frequently falls too close to CPI. Examples: June 10, 2025 is one trading day before CPI; July 10, 2025 is three trading days before; July 10, 2026 is two trading days before. October 21, 2025 is three trading days before the delayed October 24 announcement, so it is **pre-release**, not a settled post-CPI observation.

The December 1 and December 9 backfills remain blocked, as previously agreed. No CPI horizon was extended to make them fit.

## 2. Making the comparison fair

### Why the published objective alone is insufficient

The original fitting objective is an inverse-duration-weighted squared price error. Seasonal adjustment changes the prices being fitted; forward valuation also changes the date, carry, and time to maturity. An improvement in that native objective is not automatically an improvement on the original observed prices.

For this study:

1. All six variants use the **same bond universe and held-out maturities within each date and quote side**.
2. Three-fold cross-validation holds out entire maturity dates, keeping all reopenings of one maturity together. Sorted unique maturities are assigned to folds round-robin.
3. Each model is refitted on two folds and evaluated on the third.
4. Predicted adjusted/forward prices are converted back to the **original settlement clean-price basis**.
5. TIPS errors are measured in original settlement yield basis points; price errors are also retained in the downloadable observations.

Forward price is affine in settlement price. Its slope is:

```text
A = (settle_REFCPI / forward_REFCPI) * (1 + repo_rate * ACT/year_days)
```

After removing the applicable seasonal multiplier, a forward-price prediction error divided by `A` is the corresponding original-price error. Intervening coupons are already incorporated in the archived forward-price intercept.

This conversion was checked against the production forward pricer for **4,872 bond/date/side observations**. Reconstructed forward prices matched exactly at published precision; the largest finite-difference slope discrepancy was below 0.000001.

For BEI, the common-basis error is:

```text
BEI residual = original-settlement nominal residual - original-settlement TIPS residual
```

The nominal residual uses the existing nominal curve and local interpolation of nominal bond yields. Therefore BEI accuracy is **conditional on that nominal model**; these are not independent traded BEI quotes, and the nominal model itself is not cross-validated here.

### Aggregation and uncertainty

Each CPI release episode receives equal weight. Within an episode, date/side mean squared errors are averaged; the square root is taken after aggregation. Thus an episode with more snapshots does not receive extra weight.

The study performed **1,764 deterministic refits** over 588 archived curves. Confidence intervals resample whole release episodes together, with 10,000 paired bootstrap resamples and a fixed seed.

The intervals are exploratory: only 4-8 episodes fall in each regime, adjacent episodes can remain economically dependent, six candidate methods are compared, and no multiple-comparison correction is applied. Thousands of bond residuals do not turn this into thousands of independent macroeconomic experiments.

### Sensitivity checks

- Across 36 combinations of exclusion window, raw versus SA carry, 3/6/12-month trend, and +/-0.25/0.50/1.00-point neutral bands, pooled below-trend TIPS rankings favor forward / SA and above-trend rankings favor settlement / SA. Near-trend rankings change. These overlapping specifications are not 36 independent confirmations.
- **Quote side matters.** Below trend, ask-only observations favor forward / SA-decay; bid-only observations favor forward / SA. Across all regimes, ask-only favors settlement / SA-decay, while bid-only favors forward / SA.
- **Shutdown-period sensitivity matters.** Excluding release episodes from October 24, 2025 through June 10, 2026 leaves only three episodes per regime. The below-trend leader remains forward / SA, the near-trend leader remains settlement / SA-decay, but the above-trend leader changes to settlement / SA-decay: 3.63 bp versus 3.89 bp for settlement / SA.

The extended exclusion covers both disrupted releases and six-month trends whose endpoint or intervening months depend on the project's filled October 2025 CPI value. It is a conservative diagnostic, not a definitive cleaned sample.

## 4. Do BEI curves flip after CPI?

**Some do in this sample, but "often" is not established.** The answer depends on maturity range, curve variant, and whether the comparison holds calendar horizons fixed.

A downward-to-upward flip requires the slope to move from less than -2 bp to more than +2 bp. The dead band avoids counting tiny near-flat changes as flips.

For the **15 events with both observations 4-10 trading days away**, the 1-to-5-year BEI curve flips downward to upward as follows:

| Variant | Ask flips | Bid flips | Denominator |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Settlement / unadjusted | 1 | 1 | 15 events per side |
| Settlement / SA | 1 | 1 | 15 |
| Settlement / SA-decay | 1 | 1 | 15 |
| Forward / unadjusted | 1 | 1 | 15 |
| Forward / SA | 3 | 2 | 15 |
| Forward / SA-decay | 3 | 2 | 15 |

The forward / SA and SA-decay curves also have one upward-to-downward flip on the ask side. Six-month-to-two-year results are included in the release-pair CSV; they also show both directions, not a universal post-CPI steepening.

The forward date normally advances at CPI releases. Comparing a "one-year forward curve" before and after therefore changes the underlying calendar interval. I also compared price-implied rates over **the same future start and end dates**, using the post-release horizon as the common start. The 1-to-5-year forward-SA BEI downward-to-upward counts become 2/15 on each side. This establishes that some flips remain after horizon alignment, but the count is small.

The observations are days or weeks apart, so policy news, auctions, liquidity, energy prices, and other news intervene. These are descriptive event comparisons, **not a causal estimate of CPI's impact**.

### Why a flip can occur mechanically

For matched zero-coupon horizons, a useful approximation is:

```text
settlement BEI over (h + t)
    = [known log inflation over h + t * forward BEI over t] / (h + t)
```

Known inflation is heavily weighted at short maturities. An above-trend known-inflation segment can elevate short settlement BEI and make it decline with maturity even when the later forward BEI is flat or rising. A weak or negative known segment can reverse the effect.

At a release, the newly known segment, the horizon, and the market's pricing of the still-unknown segment can all change. Seasonal adjustment and a forward calculation address different pieces of this problem; neither guarantees a monotonic curve.

## 5. Should the real-yield curve always rise?

**No. There is no theoretical requirement that real yields or BEI rise monotonically with maturity.**

A real-yield curve reflects expected future real short rates, term premia, liquidity and bond-specific effects. Expectations of lower future real rates can produce an inverted real curve. BEI is inflation compensation, not pure expected inflation; inflation-risk and relative-liquidity premia also matter.

The Federal Reserve's [TIPS yield-curve explanation](https://www.federalreserve.gov/data/tips-yield-curve-and-inflation-compensation.htm) and [GSW research paper](https://www.federalreserve.gov/pubs/feds/2008/200805/) describe those distinctions. Treasury's [TIPS explanation](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/marketable-securities/tips/) also distinguishes fixed coupons, index-adjusted cash flows, and real yields.

In this archive, every retained 2-to-10-year real curve has a positive endpoint slope. But seasonally adjusted 1-to-5-year real slopes decline by more than 2 bp in roughly 6%-9% of episode-weighted observations, depending on valuation. At six months to two years, SA-decay real slopes decline about 23%-27% of the time.

Those short-end percentages need the coverage caveat below. They do not prove that inversion is either economically wrong or accurately estimated.

**Do not impose "upward sloping" as the criterion for correctness.** Diagnose implausible extrapolation or parameter instability separately from a genuinely inverted curve.

## 6. The important historical-data limitations

### Missing historical TIPS metadata

Current metadata does not represent the complete bond universe outstanding in 2025. For example, on June 2, 2025 the ask curve contains 47 TIPS and its shortest maturity is October 15, 2026, about 1.37 years away. Yet saved source quotes include positive asks for omitted January, April and July 2026 maturities.

Four positive, forward-eligible ask quotes are omitted on that date because their CUSIPs are absent from current metadata. On the bid side, six such quotes are missing. These are not simply bonds intentionally excluded because they mature before the forward date.

Across the primary sample:

- **24 of 33 dates** have at least one quote side missing forward-eligible, positively quoted TIPS because of metadata.
- **38 of 66 date/side combinations** have such missing metadata.
- Six historically quoted CUSIPs are affected: `912828N71`, `912810FS2`, `91282CCA7`, `912828S50`, `912828XL9`, `91282CAQ4`.
- **20 of 66** settlement date/side curves have no fitted bond maturing within one year.
- **53 of 66** have no fitted bond maturing within six months.

Consequently, many six-month and some one-year curve points are **extrapolations**, not interpolations backed by nearby bonds. Cash-flow fitting can constrain certain discounted combinations without reliably identifying the whole short-end zero curve.

### A concrete instability

Across the October 2025 event pair, the full-SA bid curve produces a fixed-start, one-year BEI move of about **-695 bp for settlement / SA** and **-823 bp for forward / SA**. The pre-observation is October 9 and the post-observation is November 3.

The pre-release settlement-SA parameters include a very short first decay time and large, nearly cancelling coefficients. This is consistent with a weakly identified short end: a reasonable bond-price fit can coexist with an extreme short-forward curve.

In the tight paired sample, bid-side fixed-start one-year BEI movement RMS is:

| Variant | All 15 pairs | Excluding the October 2025 pair |
|---|---:|---:|
| Settlement / SA | 180.80 bp | 22.08 bp |
| Settlement / SA-decay | 22.21 bp | 21.78 bp |
| Forward / SA | 214.26 bp | 28.57 bp |
| Forward / SA-decay | 28.82 bp | 28.78 bp |

SA-decay is less affected by this particular fit instability. That is a robustness argument for monitoring it, not proof that it always estimates the true curve better. Removing the outlier is shown transparently as a diagnostic, not silently used to select the winner.

### Other limitations

- CPI and seasonal inputs are current-vintage values. Release-date truncation prevents access to later horizons, but does not recreate every historical data revision.
- October 2025 CPI is explicitly gap-filled by the project, not an independently observed release. It affects the trend and some reference-CPI/seasonal calculations.
- Release/reference-month associations come from the project's calendar. The calendar builder infers the reference month from the release month, a convention that should be manually verified for exceptional delays or multi-month releases.
- FedInvest agency prices are not a consolidated live executable market. Some short asks are unavailable while bids exist.
- Financing uses the repository's T-bill-derived repo proxy. That is not necessarily a bond-specific executable financing rate.
- The existing model's treatment of indexation, coupon carry and the TIPS principal floor is retained; the study does not add an option-adjusted floor or liquidity model.
- Cross-validation is cross-sectional, not a genuine future-period forecast. It also uses one deterministic maturity-grouped fold layout; alternative folds and maturity-month holdouts would be useful confirmation.

## 7. Are more dates required?

### First repair coverage, not just frequency

The highest-value next action is to recover reference metadata for bonds that were outstanding historically but have since matured, and regenerate the same historical dates. This would change the information supporting the short end and could change both rankings and apparent CPI flips.

Without that repair, adding more dates can repeatedly sample the same biased bond universe.

### Within the existing period

The current archive already supplies **15 usable pre/post pairs** at 4-10 trading days from actual releases. Thus many more arbitrary monthly dates are not needed just to obtain before/after comparisons.

For uniform timing, prefer **release -5 and +5 Treasury trading days**, rather than first/seventh/21st-calendar-day rules:

- Four of the required 32 dates for 16 actual releases are already present.
- **28 additional dates** would complete that uniform 32-date design.
- **27 are potentially processable** under existing horizon rules.
- The remaining date, **December 11, 2025**, is structurally blocked because its maximum historical reference-CPI date is December 1. It must stay a documented exception under the agreed policy.

The 28 missing dates are:

```text
2025-06-04  2025-06-18  2025-07-08  2025-07-22
2025-08-05  2025-08-19  2025-09-04  2025-09-18
2025-10-17  2025-10-31  2025-12-11 [blocked]  2025-12-26
2026-01-06  2026-02-06  2026-03-04  2026-03-18
2026-04-03  2026-04-17  2026-05-05  2026-05-19
2026-06-03  2026-06-17  2026-07-07  2026-08-05
2026-08-19  2026-09-03  2026-09-18  2026-10-06
```

April 3, 2026 is an early-close trading day under the Treasury-market calendar used here.

Adding these dates would standardize event timing and provide a useful check on sensitivity to the particular observation day. **It would not increase the number of independent CPI releases.** No additional reports were generated for this analysis.

### Across more CPI episodes

The current regime counts are just **5 below, 4 near and 8 above**. That is enough for a pilot study, not strong three-regime conclusions.

An illustrative paired-episode power calculation is:

```text
required episodes ~= (1.96 + 0.84)^2 * SD(paired RMSE differences)^2 / target_difference^2
```

For pooled forward-SA versus settlement-SA-decay, the pilot SD is about 2.06 bp for all-maturity TIPS RMSE and 3.82 bp for short-TIPS RMSE. Detecting a **1 bp** average paired advantage at approximately 80% power would suggest roughly **34 episodes overall** or **115 episodes for the short end**, before allowing for macroeconomic dependence, multiple comparisons or data reconstruction uncertainty.

These estimates concern average paired event-level RMSE differences; they are approximate planning calculations, not a power guarantee for the nonlinear pooled RMSE statistic. Very small pilot SDs from four observations must not be interpreted as adequate evidence with one or two episodes.

Practical design:

1. Treat **20-30 well-covered releases per regime** as a useful initial target, not a guaranteed sufficient sample.
2. Thirty per regime means 90 releases in total, at least 7.5 years of monthly announcements if perfectly balanced; rare regimes require longer.
3. Extend backward across several inflation environments after recovering historical metadata and, ideally, point-in-time CPI/seasonal vintages.
4. Pre-specify economically meaningful differences, such as 1 bp overall and perhaps 2 bp at the short end, rather than trying to prove a 0.03 bp advantage.
5. Add a second remote observation, for example -10/+10 trading days, only if needed to estimate within-regime day-to-day variation. Do not count it as another CPI event.
6. Hold out complete release episodes, and eventually complete years, when evaluating a regime-dependent choice rule.

## 8. Recommended interpretation now

- **Unadjusted curves:** retain as transparent raw-pricing diagnostics, not the leading smooth-curve estimator in this sample.
- **SA and SA-decay:** both deserve consideration; seasonal adjustment has the clearest empirical benefit.
- **Forward versus settlement:** show both. They answer different economic questions, even after their estimation errors are compared on a common-price basis.
- **Working display default:** settlement / SA-decay is a defensible robustness-oriented starting view, alongside forward / SA-decay as a known-carry-stripped comparison. This is a practical monitoring recommendation, not a demonstrated universal accuracy winner.
- **Regime switching:** do not yet automate "forward SA below trend, settlement SA above trend." The rule is selected on a small retrospective sample, changes with quote side and data exclusions, and has not been evaluated on untouched release episodes.
- **Priority:** repair historical metadata, standardize release-relative sampling, and rerun the study before making strong claims about short-term curve shape or real-return superiority.
